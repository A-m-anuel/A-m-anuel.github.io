Why I keep a public portfolio

A CV lists what I have done. This site shows it.

I wanted one link I could share on LinkedIn, GitHub and job applications without making people search across several platforms. A personal site answers the important questions quickly: who I am, what kind of security work I care about, and where the actual work lives.

In security, claims are cheap and evidence matters. So the site puts the evidence first: open-source tools you can read and run, the certifications behind my experience, and writing that shows how I think through a problem.

It is also a commitment to keep building in public. Every new tool or write-up ends up here, which keeps me honest about shipping things rather than only collecting ideas.
